On Time

Why We Arrive Late—and How to Stop

The meeting starts at nine. You know the route by heart. So why are you still apologizing at 9:07?

On Time starts with stories. A Tokyo train company apologized for a train that left twenty seconds early. A team of experts took eight years to write a textbook they had expected to finish in two. A bullet-train crew cleans a whole car in seven minutes, every time. Seminary students walked past a man in need because they were in a hurry. Zahir Hasan turns each of these stories into a practical model you can use the very next morning.

The book contains eleven step-by-step models, among them the Five-Trip Ledger, the Door-to-Room Map, the Exit Sequence, the Two-Cue System, the Backward Clock, Tripwires, and the Useful Delay Message. They show you how to measure a real trip rather than the one you remember, get out of the door without the last-minute scramble, and handle the bad days honestly.

Inside you will also find a seven-day plan, rules for trains, cars, flights and video calls, a household departure plan, a troubleshooting guide, ready-to-use messages, and a departure card to keep by the door.

It is a short, practical guide for anyone tired of arriving with an apology.
