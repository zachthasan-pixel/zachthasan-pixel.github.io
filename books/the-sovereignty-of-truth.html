The Sovereignty of Truth

How Democracies Legislate Against Political Deception

"When a lie can reach millions before the truth wakes up, who should hold the emergency brake on political deception—and at what cost to free speech?"

The Sovereignty of Truth traces the most consequential legal experiment of our time: the scramble by the world's democracies to defend the shared facts that self-government depends on, without becoming the censors they fear.

Writing with clarity and hard-won balance, Zahir Hasan follows the story across three continents—from Germany's pioneering NetzDG and France's election courts, to Europe's sweeping Digital Services Act and its first €120-million fine against X; from America's First Amendment refusal to regulate, to Brazil's astonishing month-long shutdown of a global platform; from Australia's abandoned misinformation bill to the dawning age of AI deepfakes that can now forge a leader's voice in seconds.

Inside this book:

• Why "misinformation," "disinformation," and "malinformation" demand three different legal answers

• How engagement algorithms, dark ads, foreign operations, and a $6-billion advertising economy quietly manufacture falsehood at scale

• The real record of Europe's laws—what worked, what backfired, and what they cost free expression

• The 2024–25 U.S. Supreme Court trilogy that redrew the limits of online-speech regulation

• Why the coming wave of synthetic media may outrun every law now on the books

This is not a partisan tract or a doom-loop. It is a clear-eyed map of how free societies are trying to keep truth sovereign—not by decree, but by design—and a sober guide to the choices ahead.

Essential reading for anyone who cares about democracy, law, technology, and the future of an open society.

Zahir Hasan (MPPA, LLM, EdD) writes on law, public policy, and democratic institutions from Tokyo.
