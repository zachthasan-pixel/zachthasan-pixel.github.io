Canada’s European Option

Associate Membership, Sovereignty, and Strategy in a Fragmenting World

On September 16, 2026, the European Commission president proposed opening the door for Canada to become the European Union’s first associate member. The invitation was historic, but the new status came without a rulebook. What would Canada gain? What obligations would it accept? And can it diversify without damaging its indispensable relationship with the United States?

Canada’s European Option provides a clear, evidence-based answer. Zahir Hasan examines the legal routes to association, the unfinished work of CETA, and Canada’s growing participation in Horizon Europe, the EU’s SAFE defence instrument, and European security forums. He then turns to the practical agenda: trade, energy, critical minerals, artificial intelligence, quantum technology, the Arctic, defence production, mobility, infrastructure, and the political obstacles within both Canada and the EU.

The book recommends a staged Atlantic Association built on reciprocal access, explicit obligations, parliamentary oversight, provincial and Indigenous participation, and measurable results. A closing chapter tests that recommendation against the alternatives, the strongest objections, and the costs, and states the conditions under which the advice should change. It rejects both dependence on one partner and the illusion that Europe can replace the United States. The path forward is strategic pluralism: stronger capacity at home and several durable partnerships abroad.

Timely and practical, this book is for readers seeking to understand how a new Canada-EU relationship could reshape trade, security, and sovereignty in a fragmenting world.
