Japan’s Unavoidable Choice

Immigration, Artificial Intelligence, and the Struggle to Remain a Global Power

Can Japan remain a major global power while its population and workforce steadily decline?

Japan faces a defining choice. It can continue limiting permanent immigration and place its faith in artificial intelligence, robotics, and a smaller but more productive society. Or it can reconsider how foreign workers, long-term residents, and future citizens fit into the nation’s economic and strategic future.

Japan’s Unavoidable Choice examines the relationship between demographic decline, immigration policy, artificial intelligence, care shortages, public finance, regional contraction, and national security. It also considers a central geopolitical question: whether Japan can preserve its influence as China becomes more powerful in Asia and throughout the world.

Drawing on Japanese government data, international institutions, and peer-reviewed research, the book explores:

Why Japan’s population decline cannot be solved by technology alone? What robots and artificial intelligence can—and cannot—replace? How temporary foreign-worker programs affect productivity and human rights? What Japan can learn from South Korea, Taiwan, Germany, Canada, and Singapore? How demographic decline affects defense, alliances, and competition with China? Four possible versions of Japan in 2050.

The book concludes with a practical ten-year strategy combining automation, domestic labor reform, managed immigration, fiscal adjustment, and carefully planned regional adaptation.

A timely and evidence-based examination of what Japan must change to preserve what matters most.
