Critical Thinking in the Age of AI

How to Question, Verify, and Decide in a World of Generative Machines

The machines can write. The question is whether we can still think.

Artificial intelligence now produces essays, forecasts, diagnoses, images, and legal-sounding arguments faster than anyone can check them. The result is a world where fluent answers are nearly free — and finding out whether an answer deserves belief costs as much as it ever did. This is the new problem of knowing, and it cannot be solved by either blind trust or blanket suspicion.

In Critical Thinking in the Age of AI, educator and researcher Dr. Zahir Hasan shows how the classic disciplines of critical thinking must be redesigned for a world of generative machines. Drawing on research in cognitive psychology, AI evaluation, and governance — and on years of university teaching — he offers a practical framework for questioning, verifying, and deciding when the source of information is a probabilistic system rather than a person.

Across twenty chapters you will learn how to:

• Separate fluency from truth, and polish from evidence

• Catch fabricated citations, invented statistics, and synthetic media before they catch you

• Recognize how your own biases interact with machine confidence

• Delegate to AI without surrendering the skills you need to supervise it

• Apply a six-stage method — and the VERIFY checklist — to any consequential AI-assisted decision

• Build classrooms and workplaces where human judgment stays in charge

With practice prompts in every section, workshop questions in every chapter, and ready-to-use templates for disclosure and verification, this is both a guide for individual readers and a course-ready text for educators and professionals.

The future of critical thinking will not be decided by what machines can say. It will be decided by what people continue to ask, verify, and refuse to hand over.
