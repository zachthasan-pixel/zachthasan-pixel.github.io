Brand of Gold

She stole a dragon's soul. Now it will not let her go.

Rook Ithara owes the Pale Ledger three lives, and the Ledger does not recalculate. So when a fence offers her a seal that can clear a life-account, she takes the job. One relic. One night. Out before the third bell.

The relic is a Heartscale, and it brands her — to a dying black wyrm with silver wire in her throat, and to the last unbound rider alive. A man Rook has never met, who wants her gone by morning and needs her breathing by dark. Two souls can hold a dragon. Three can command a flight. The brand lasts until one of the bound three dies.

Beneath the city of Vesperhold, twenty-six dragons wait in stalls of black iron, clipped and branded and milked for heat. High Warden Orsel wants a third soul on the binding, and with it every wyrm in the empire on one chain. He is courteous about it. He is patient. He is not wrong about the arithmetic — the pipes run warm because the animals are chained, and eleven thousand people sleep over that nave.

Rook does not work for free. She does not kneel. And the only thing standing between a city and a leash is a thief who has never been able to stop counting.

Book One of the Scale and Sorrow trilogy. A dark fantasy romance with a forced soul-bond, a morally grey antagonist who argues in good faith, and a dragon who is nobody's mount. Explicit content. For readers 18+.
