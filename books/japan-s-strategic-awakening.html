Japan's Strategic Awakening

Law, Capability, and the Realignment of Indo-Pacific Security

Can a nation built for peace defend it? For nearly eighty years, Japan kept the peace through a careful bargain: a pacifist constitution, American protection, and a stable Asia. That bargain is breaking. Chinese coast guard fleets circle disputed islands. North Korean missiles arc over Japanese towns. Russian and Chinese warships sail the archipelago in company. And in Washington, old certainties are giving way to hard bargaining.

Japan's Strategic Awakening tells the story of how the world's most successful pacifist democracy is answering: record defense budgets, counterstrike missiles,

historic warship exports to Australia, a lattice of new partnerships across the Indo-Pacific—and a constitutional question that can no longer be deferred.

Writing from Tokyo, Dr. Zahir Hasan examines the law, the capabilities, and the alliances behind Japan's transformation, and argues that peace endures only when all three align. Includes a chronology, glossary, discussion questions for classroom use, and full APA references. Essential reading for students of international relations, policy professionals, and anyone who wants to understand the future of Asia.
