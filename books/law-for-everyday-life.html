Law for Everyday Life

A Practical Guide to Contracts, Employment, Privacy, Consumer Rights, and the Internet

The lease on the table. The job you can leave. The charge that will not cancel. The post that will not come down. Ordinary life runs on rules most people never see until something goes wrong.

Law for Everyday Life explains, in plain language, how those rules actually work: contracts and the fine print behind every click, wages and overtime, security deposits and evictions, refunds and chargebacks, personal data, online defamation, copyright in the age of AI, and what really happens when you sue someone or get sued yourself. Each chapter ends with a worked example showing how a real dispute plays out, and the appendices supply the tools: document checklists, sample letters you can adapt, a glossary, quick answers to common questions, and a one-hour annual legal health check.

Written for people who live inside contracts, not courtrooms, this book teaches the habit that wins disputes: know the rule, know the deadline, and keep the paper. It is a practical guide, not legal advice, with comparative notes on Japan for readers whose lives cross borders.
