Take Back Your Hours

Simple Ways to Manage Your Time in a Distracted World

Where does your day actually go?

The average person now spends three to five hours a day on a phone — more than forty full days a year — and most of it vanishes without leaving anything behind. You are not lazy, and you are not broken. You are living in the most distracting environment in human history, and nobody handed you a manual for it.

This is the manual. In twenty short chapters, each ending with one simple thing to try today, you will learn how to find the hours you didn’t know you had: silence the notifications that interrupt you dozens of times a day, choose the one thing that makes each day count, tame the endless feeds, protect your evenings and the people you love — and restart gently when it all falls apart, as it sometimes will.

No apps to buy. No complicated system. Just simple, human-sized changes, backed by real research and drawn from years of teaching in classrooms on three continents. Follow three relatable companions — a manager drowning in email, a nurse with two young children, and a graduate student whose evenings kept disappearing — as they take their hours back. Then take back yours.
