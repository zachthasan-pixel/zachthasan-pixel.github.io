The Loss of Goodwill

Israel, Gaza, and the Crisis of Moral Legitimacy

The Loss of Goodwill is a documented history and legal analysis of how a state founded on the moral urgency of Jewish refuge came to face allegations of apartheid, forced displacement, and genocide.

Zahir Hasan traces the arc from the Holocaust and the founding of Israel through the Nakba, the 1967 occupation, Oslo, settlement and de facto annexation, the blockade of Gaza, the atrocities of October 7, 2023, and the war that followed. This edition carries the record through the ceasefire of October 2025 and its unfinished aftermath, the regional war fought in Lebanon, Syria, and Iran, the arms restrictions and trade measures imposed by Israel’s own allies, and the argument now under way inside Jewish communities about what memory requires.

The book is built on primary sources: orders and advisory opinions of the International Court of Justice, warrants and rulings of the International Criminal Court, United Nations investigations, peer-reviewed mortality studies, Israeli government arguments in their own words, archival scholarship, and international polling. It separates documented fact from allegation, provisional measures from final judgments, and legal finding from moral judgment.

It condemns Hamas’s crimes, rejects collective blame and Nazi analogies, and insists that Jewish safety and Palestinian freedom are not rival claims to humanity. It asks a harder question: can security remain legitimate when it depends on another people’s permanent subordination?
